## 3. Mathematical Method

For RSA modulus n = p*q, if two moduli share a prime factor, their GCD can reveal it. For a factored key, q = n/p, phi(n) = (p-1)(q-1), and d is the modular inverse of e modulo phi(n). The script records these values for detected toy-key findings.

## 4. Batch-GCD Algorithm and Complexity

The implementation constructs a balanced product tree, then propagates remainders to compute the total product modulo each n_i squared. It then calculates g_i = gcd(n_i, (M mod n_i^2)/n_i). Non-trivial factors identify moduli that share a factor.

A naive all-pairs approach performs O(N^2) GCD comparisons. The assignment's target for product/remainder trees is O(N log^2 N) arithmetic operations under its stated analysis. Actual bit complexity and runtime depend on modulus bit length and the big-integer multiplication algorithm; this small-input benchmark is not a realistic RSA-sized performance guarantee.

## 7. Remediation and Entropy Mitigations

- Revoke and replace keys confirmed to be compromised; do not reuse their private keys.
- Generate keys with a vetted cryptographic library and a properly seeded CSPRNG.
- Validate CSPRNG initialization and key-generation health checks before generating keys.
- Ensure sufficient entropy is available at boot; use an appropriate entropy pool and avoid generating keys before the random-number generator is ready.
- Avoid cloning devices or virtual-machine images that preserve identical random state.
- Audit related key inventories for shared factors and verify replacement keys before deployment.
- Restrict access to private keys, rotate exposed credentials, and retain audit logs.
